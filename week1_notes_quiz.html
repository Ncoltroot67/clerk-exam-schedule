📋 Study Notes — CPL Art. 1 Definitions (corrected & updated per official NY Legislature text)
§1.00 – §1.10 — Basics
§1.00 CPL = Criminal Procedure Law
§1.10 Applies exclusively to ALL criminal actions, appeals, and post-judgment proceedings
§1.20 — The 7 Accusatory Instruments
Filing of an accusatory instrument commences a proceeding in Criminal Court. The 7 types:
1Indictment (including reduced indictment)
2Information
3Simplified Information
4Prosecutor's Information
5Superior Court Information
6Misdemeanor Complaint
7Felony Complaint
🆕 A parking infraction appearance ticket (verified, containing plate/type/expiration/make/model/body type) also qualifies as an accusatory instrument [§1.20(1)(b)]
§1.20(1) All instruments: State = plaintiff, entitled "The People of the State of New York"
§1.20(2) Courts split into: Superior Courts (Indictments + Superior Court Information ONLY) and Local Criminal Courts (all others)
§1.20(3–8) — Each Instrument Explained
Indictment §1.20(3) Written by a GRAND JURY — at least one crime, may include other offenses
Superior Court Info §1.20(3) Same as indictment but from District Attorney — same force and effect
⚡ OFFENSE = any act punishable by fine/imprisonment. CRIME = misdemeanors + felonies ONLY
Information §1.20(4) Verified by an individual — at least one offense, NO FELONIES — both commences AND serves as basis for prosecution
Simplified Info §1.20(5) THREE types: (a) Traffic — traffic infraction or misdemeanor (b) Parks — non-felony under Parks/Navigation Law (c) Environmental Conservation — non-felony under ECL. All three: commence AND basis for prosecution
Prosecutor's Info §1.20(6) By District Attorney — non-felony offenses only
Misdemeanor Complaint §1.20(7) At least one misdemeanor, no felonies — commences action; prosecution only upon defendant's consent
Felony Complaint §1.20(8) Local Criminal Court — felonies only — commences action but NOT basis for prosecution
§1.20(9–18) — Key Procedural Definitions
Arraignment §1.20(9) Appearance before Court — court acquires control over defendant, sets bail, assigns counsel, schedules proceedings
Jury Trial §1.20(11) Commences with jury selection. Non-jury trial commences with first opening address, or if none, first witness
Verdict §1.20(12) Jury's announcement OR Court's decision
Conviction §1.20(13) When plea or verdict is entered — EXCEPT regarding a Felony Complaint
Judgment §1.20(14-15) = Conviction + Sentence
Criminal Action §1.20(16-17) Commences with filing of first accusatory instrument — continues until sentencing/disposition
Trial Jurisdiction §1.20(24-25) Proper venue to file + authority to take plea or enter verdict. Otherwise = Preliminary Jurisdiction only
§1.20(26–30) — Process & Warrants
Appearance Ticket §1.20(26) Written notice by a public servant — directs appearance for accusatory instrument not yet filed
Summons §1.20(27) Process of the court itself (local OR superior) — directs appearance for arraignment — only issued after action has commenced
🆕 Summons may now be issued by SUPERIOR COURT as well as local criminal court
Warrant of Arrest §1.20(28) Issued by Local Criminal Courts ONLY — must be executed by POLICE
Superior Court Warrant §1.20(29) Superior Court — same effect as warrant of arrest
Bench Warrant §1.20(30) For previously arraigned defendants in pending cases — directed to police officer OR uniformed court officer per §530.70(b)
§1.20(31–45) — People, Officers & Special Definitions
Prosecutor §1.20(31-32) = District Attorney + Attorney General + deputies + Special Prosecutor/IG for People with Special Needs + IG for Transportation
Peace Officer §1.20(33) = Persons listed in CPL §2.10
Police §1.20(34) 17 categories including: State/County/City officers, Sheriffs (outside NYC ONLY), DA investigators, NYC Fire Marshals, LIRR police, State University Police, Forest Rangers, and more
⚡ NYC Sheriffs = civil jurisdiction ONLY. NOT police officers under CPL
Petty Offense §1.20(39) = Violation OR Traffic Infraction (NOT misdemeanors)
Juvenile Offender §1.20(42) Age 13: Murder 2 or Sexually Motivated Felony only. Age 14-15: broader list including burglary, robbery, assault 1, rape 1 etc. Age 14: firearm possession only if on school grounds
🆕 Adolescent Offender §1.20(44): Age 16 (offenses on/after Oct 1, 2018) or Age 17 (on/after Oct 1, 2019) charged with felony — processed through Youth Part (Raise the Age legislation)
🆕 Expunge §1.20(45): Arrest deemed nullity — records marked expunged or destroyed per CPL §160.50 — person restored to pre-arrest status
Lesser-Included Offense §1.20(37) Cannot commit greater offense without simultaneously committing the lesser. All attempts are automatically lesser-included offenses
❓ Quiz — CPL Art. 1 (43 questions)
Q1
CPL applies to which criminal actions and proceedings?
All criminal actions and proceedings commenced before but still pending on the effective date of CPL, provided application would not work injustice. Effective date: September 1, 1971.
Q2
A "superior court information" is similar in legal effect to an indictment, but is filed by whom?
A district attorney. An indictment is the product of a grand jury; a superior court information has the same force and effect but is filed by a DA.
Q3
An information in a local criminal court is:
A verified written accusation by a person. Verification means "sworn to" per CPL 100.30.
Q4
An information may serve:
Both to commence a criminal action AND as a basis for prosecution.
Q5
A "prosecutor's information" can charge a defendant with a:
Traffic infraction only. A prosecutor's information cannot be used for a felony.
Q6
A misdemeanor complaint can charge one or more defendants with:
Any offense, with at least one being a crime, but none being a felony.
Q7
A felony complaint…
Can serve to commence a criminal action but may NOT serve as a basis for prosecution. Only an indictment or superior court information can serve as the basis for prosecuting a felony. (CPL 1.20-8)
Q8
Arraignment refers to a defendant's appearance in:
The court in which the criminal action is pending. Held for the court to acquire control over the defendant's person, set bail status, assign counsel, and schedule further proceedings. (CPL 1.20-9)
Q9
A jury trial commences:
With selection of the jury. (CPL 40.30-1(b) — significant for double jeopardy purposes.)
Q10
A defendant arrested and charged with a felony demands a jury trial. Which of the following is part of the jury trial?
Selection of jurors. A jury trial commences with selection and includes all proceedings through rendition of verdict. Sentence follows conviction and is NOT part of the jury trial.
Q11
Which one of the following constitutes a conviction?
The entry of a plea of guilty upon an indictment. Imposition of sentence is subsequent to conviction. A plea of guilty upon a felony complaint is expressly negated by CPL 1.20-13.
Q12
Accusatory instruments are used on behalf of the State as plaintiff to charge:
All offenses. An offense means any conduct for which a sentence of imprisonment or fine is provided.
Q13
According to CPL, a criminal action commences when:
The first accusatory instrument is filed against the defendant. Not at arrest, not at the crime date, not at indictment.
Q14
What does "trial jurisdiction" mean?
The authority of a court to make a final disposition of a case on its merits. A court can have both preliminary and trial jurisdiction.
Q15
Regarding an appearance ticket — which statement is correct? (1) Must be in writing (2) May be issued only by a police officer (3) May require defendant to appear in any criminal court (4) Cannot be used for a felony
Only statement 1 is correct. #2 wrong — other public servants may issue per CPL 150.10. #3 wrong — directs appearance in a designated LOCAL criminal court only. #4 wrong — may be used for certain class E felonies per CPL 150.20-2.
Q16
In a criminal action, a "summons" is served upon:
A defendant against whom a criminal action has commenced. Witnesses are summoned by subpoena. Summons is based on a filed accusatory instrument.
Q17
A process of a local criminal court requiring a defendant to appear for arraignment upon a filed accusatory instrument is known as:
A summons. An appearance ticket is issued by a police officer/public servant — not a court process. A bench warrant produces a defendant for a purpose other than initial arraignment.
Q18
A summons may be issued by:
A local or superior court. Effective 11/1/93, superior courts were authorized to issue a summons. A police officer issues an appearance ticket, not a summons.
Q19
Police apply to a local criminal court for an order directing a police officer to arrest Joe for arraignment upon a filed accusatory instrument. This order is called a:
Warrant of arrest. A bench warrant produces a defendant for some purpose other than initial arraignment. A search warrant is directed at physical evidence, not a defendant.
Q20
A warrant of arrest is addressed to and can be executed by:
A police officer. Not all peace officers. "Court officer" title does not exist per CPL.
Q21
To bring a defendant before the court for arraignment upon an indictment, an officer must obtain:
A superior court warrant of arrest. A regular warrant of arrest has no application to an indictment per CPL 1.20-27, 130.10. A bench warrant is used when a defendant has already been arraigned.
Q22
A "superior court warrant of arrest" directs __________ to arrest a defendant for arraignment in a superior court.
A police officer. Not any peace officer, not a county marshal. Note: DA investigators are police officers per CPL 1.20-34(g).
Q23
A process directing a police officer to take into custody a defendant who has previously been arraigned upon an accusatory instrument is:
A bench warrant. Since the defendant has previously been arraigned, a bench warrant is appropriate to achieve further court appearance.
Q24
Accusatory instruments include:
Misdemeanor complaints and indictments. A warrant of arrest is NOT an accusatory instrument — it is based on one per CPL 120.10-1.
Q25
The purpose of a bench warrant is to:
Produce a defendant in court on a pending criminal action for some purpose other than initial arraignment.
Q26
The term "prosecutor" includes:
A district attorney. The attorney general and special prosecutors may represent the people, but "prosecutor" refers to the head of the DA's office. NYC Corporation Counsel handles civil matters only.
Q27
Which of the following are police officers under CPL?
An investigator employed in the office of a district attorney. Sheriffs have police officer status only when employed by counties OUTSIDE NYC. In NYC, sheriffs exercise civil jurisdiction only.
Q28
The State University of New York appoints security persons designated in CPL as:
Police officers.
Q29
Select the correct statement as to the geographical area of employment of police officers:
A 20-year NYS police officer assigned to one station has a geographical area of employment of the entire state. NYC officers = entire city. Deputy sheriff = the county. Town officer = includes county and state roads through the town. Note: subdivision 34-a re NYS geographical area repealed eff. 9/1/25.
Q30
Select the correct statement as to the geographical area of employment of a police officer appointed by the state university:
University campuses and other university property, including any portion of a public highway which may cross or abut university property. (CPL 1.20-34a(d))
Q31
Select all correct statements as to a lesser included offense:
In each situation it would be impossible to commit the greater offense without at the same time committing the lesser. Petit larceny is NOT a lesser included of grand larceny 1st. Trespass IS a lesser included of burglary 1st. Intentional assault IS a lesser included of intentional murder.
Q32
An attempt to commit intentional assault was successful. Defendant agrees to plead guilty to attempted assault in full satisfaction of the charge. This guilty plea:
Can be accepted, although not included in the indictment. A defendant may plead guilty to a lesser included offense per CPL 220.10. The lesser offense need not be charged in the indictment.
Q33
According to CPL, an affirmation is most nearly equal to:
An oath. An affirmation may be used by persons who have personal reasons for refusing to take an oath.
Q34
Petty offenses include:
Traffic infractions and violations. Petty offense does NOT include misdemeanors.
Q35
Which of the following constitutes a juvenile offender?
A fourteen-year-old who commits acts constituting burglary first degree. A 13-year-old committing rape is NOT a juvenile offender. A 12-year-old cannot be criminally responsible for any crime.
Q36
As to possession of a loaded firearm by a person fourteen years of age, select the correct statement:
There is criminal responsibility only if such possession is on school grounds. Legislature extended criminal responsibility for a 14-year-old only for conduct under PL 265.02-4 or PL 265.03 occurring on school grounds as defined in PL 220.00-14.
Q37
The State is designated as the plaintiff in every accusatory instrument:
Without exception. In a civil action the victim may be the plaintiff; but in a criminal action the State enforces criminal law on behalf of "the people."
Q38
An appearance ticket or parking infraction must contain: I. Time and place II. License plate III. Make and model IV. Body type — which are required?
I, II, III, and IV — all four. CPL 1.20(1)(b) requires all four elements.
Q39
"Local criminal court accusatory instruments" include:
Felony complaints. Indictments and superior court informations are excluded — those are used only in superior courts.
Q40
An indictment is an accusation by:
A grand jury. Neither a prosecutor, judge, nor police officer has the power to indict.
Q41
Which of the following relate to an indictment? 1. Written accusation 2. Accusation by a DA 3. Filed with a superior court 4. Only concerns felonies
1 and 3 are correct. #2 wrong — indictment is by a grand jury, not a DA. #4 wrong — an indictment must include at least one crime, which may be a misdemeanor.
Q42
An indictment is filed with a superior court in order to:
Charge one or more defendants with a crime. An indictment is the product of grand jury consideration — it does not alert the grand jury. Most indictments follow a felony complaint that already commenced the action.
Q43
A superior court information is classified in CPL as:
A written accusation by a district attorney, filed with a superior court, charging a defendant with one or more offenses, at least one of which is a crime. Under CPL Article 195, with defendant's consent, a DA may file this as an alternative to grand jury action — same force and effect as an indictment.
