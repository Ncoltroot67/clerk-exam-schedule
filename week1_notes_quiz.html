📋 Study Notes — CPL Art. 10 · Criminal Courts: Enumeration, Definitions & Jurisdiction (2026)
§10.10 — The Two Tiers of Criminal Courts
Superior Courts: Supreme Court OR County Court — ONLY these two
Local Criminal Courts: District Court · NYC Criminal Court · City Court · Town Court · Village Court · Supreme Court Justice sitting as LCC · County Judge sitting as LCC
⚡ NYC Criminal Court ≠ City Court. NYC Criminal Court is its own LCC category. City Court = any city court OTHER than NYC, having trial jurisdiction of offenses less than felony grade only
Town Court = all the town justices of a town. Village Court = village justice(s) — if all absent, an authorized Associate Justice may act
Dual-jurisdiction courts — when exercising civil jurisdiction only, NOT acting as a criminal court. Orders made in civil capacity are NOT orders of a criminal court
§10.20 — Superior Courts: Jurisdiction
Trial jurisdiction — Felonies: EXCLUSIVE — only Superior Courts try felonies
Trial jurisdiction — Misdemeanors: CONCURRENT — shared with Local Criminal Courts
Trial jurisdiction — Petty Offenses: ONLY when charged in an indictment that ALSO charges a crime
⚡ Superior Court preliminary jurisdiction = Grand Jury ONLY. Superior Courts exercise preliminary jurisdiction only through their Grand Juries — not directly
Superior Court judge sitting as LCC: Limited to (1) conducting arraignments, (2) issuing warrants of arrest, (3) issuing search warrants — PRELIMINARY JURISDICTION ONLY, no trial jurisdiction
§10.30 — Local Criminal Courts: Jurisdiction
Felonies: Preliminary jurisdiction ONLY — subject to divestiture. NO trial jurisdiction
Misdemeanors: CONCURRENT trial jurisdiction — subject to divestiture by Superior Court in any particular case
Petty Offenses: EXCLUSIVE trial jurisdiction — except when petty offense charged alongside a crime in a Superior Court indictment
Preliminary jurisdiction: All offenses — subject to divestiture by superior courts and their grand juries
⚡ Divestiture: Superior Court can divest an LCC of misdemeanor jurisdiction in a particular case. LCC preliminary jurisdiction is always subject to divestiture
Superior Court judge sitting as LCC: NO trial jurisdiction of ANY offense — preliminary jurisdiction ONLY regardless of what offense is charged
§10.40 — Chief Administrator: Forms and E-Filing
Forms: Chief Administrator may adopt/amend/rescind forms. Failure to use authorized forms is NOT grounds alone to deny or grant any motion
E-filing: Chief Administrator may promulgate e-filing rules with approval of Administrative Board of Courts — for both commencing actions and pending proceedings
Voluntary program: No consent of other party needed to file. No party may be compelled to participate
Mandatory program: Chief Administrator must first obtain agreement of DA AND Criminal Defense Bar of the county. Reasonable notice and comment opportunity required. Comments posted publicly on OCA website
⚡ Unrepresented parties (pro se): ALWAYS exempt from e-filing — whether voluntary or mandatory. Cannot be compelled to participate under any circumstances
⚡ Criminal e-filed documents are NEVER publicly available online. No paper filed electronically in a criminal proceeding is available for public inspection online. Chief Administrator may post unsealed docs on a separate public UCS website only when in the public interest
Attorney opt-out (mandatory programs): May opt out by certifying lack of computer hardware/internet/conversion device OR lack of requisite knowledge. Court may also exempt for good cause. Knowledge of attorney's employees is imputed to the attorney
Consent of Criminal Defense Bar: Written consent from all provider offices/organizations in the county that represented 25% or more of persons represented by public defense providers (per County Law §722 annual reports)

📋 Study Notes — CPL Art. 30 · Timeliness of Prosecutions & Speedy Trial (2026)
§30.10 — Standard Limitation Periods
⚡ Class A Felony = NO LIMIT. Rape 1° · Criminal Sexual Act 1° · Aggravated Sexual Abuse 1° · Course of Sexual Conduct Against Child 1° · Incest 1° = all NO LIMIT
🆕 NEW 2026 — Rape 2°/CSA 2° (subd. 2) + Incest 2° where underlying act is Rape 2° or CSA 2°: 20 years from commission OR 10 years from first report to law enforcement — whichever is EARLIER
🆕 NEW 2026 — Rape 3°/CSA 3° (subds. 1 or 3): 10 years from commission
Any other Felony: 5 years · Misdemeanor: 2 years · Petty Offense: 1 year
§30.10(3) — Extended Limitation Periods
(a) Larceny by Fiduciary: 1 year from discovery (or when discoverable by reasonable diligence)
(b) Public Officer Misconduct: Any time during service OR up to 5 years after termination — but NOT more than 5 years beyond the normal limitation period
⚡ Public officer: double cap — 5 years after leaving office AND never more than 5 years beyond standard period
(e) Course of Sexual Conduct Against Child 2°: 5 years from most recent act
🆕 NEW 2026 — §30.10(3)(f): Child sex offenses — tolling period runs until child turns AGE 23 (previously 18), OR offense is reported to law enforcement/SCR — whichever comes FIRST
(g) Terrorism Felonies (PL Art. 490): 8 years. If death or foreseeable risk of death/serious physical injury: NO LIMIT
🆕 NEW 2026 — §30.10(3)(h): Deed Theft / Fraud in real property transaction (Felony): 5 years from commission OR 2 years from discovery — whichever is LATER (note: LATER, not earlier)
§30.10(4) — Tolling Provisions
(a) Defendant continuously outside NYS, OR whereabouts continuously unknown and unascertainable by reasonable diligence — tolling cannot extend the period by more than 5 additional years
(b) Period from commencement of prior prosecution on related charges to dismissal (where dismissal permits re-filing)
§30.30(1) — Readiness Deadlines (motion to dismiss MUST be granted)
⚡ (a) At least one FELONY charged → 6 months from commencement
⚡ (b) At least one MISDEMEANOR punishable by imprisonment of MORE THAN 3 months, no felony → 90 days
⚡ (c) At least one MISDEMEANOR punishable by imprisonment of NOT MORE THAN 3 months, no crime punishable by more than 3 months → 60 days
⚡ (d) At least one VIOLATION, no crime → 30 days
§30.30(1)(e): For purposes of subdivision 1, the term "offense" includes Vehicle and Traffic Law infractions
§30.30(2) — Custody Release Deadlines (defendant in custody must be released)
⚡ (a) At least one FELONY → 90 days from commencement of commitment to custody of sheriff or OCFS
⚡ (b) At least one MISDEMEANOR punishable by MORE THAN 3 months, no felony → 30 days
⚡ (c) At least one MISDEMEANOR punishable by NOT MORE THAN 3 months, no crime punishable by more than 3 months → 15 days
⚡ (d) At least one VIOLATION, no crime → 5 days
§30.30(2)(e): For purposes of subdivision 2, the term "offense" includes Vehicle and Traffic Law infractions
§30.30(3) — Exceptions
(a) Homicide exemption: Subdivisions 1 AND 2 do NOT apply to criminal actions where defendant is accused of offenses defined in PL §§125.10, 125.15, 125.20, 125.25, 125.26, and 125.27
(b) Prior readiness exception: A motion to dismiss MAY be denied even after time expires if — People WERE previously ready AND current unreadiness is due to exceptional fact or circumstance (e.g. sudden unavailability of material evidence) AND DA exercised due diligence AND reasonable grounds to believe evidence will become available in a reasonable period
(c) Custody release — shall NOT apply when:
(i) Defendant is serving a term of imprisonment for another offense
(ii) Defendant is also held pending trial of another criminal charge as to which the applicable period has not yet elapsed
(iii) Defendant was released and re-detained after being charged with another crime or violating conditions of release — including failure to appear at a judicial proceeding
§30.30(4) — Excluded Periods (Do NOT Count Against People's Clock)
(a) Reasonable period of delay from other proceedings concerning defendant — including: competency determinations · period of incompetency · demand to produce · bill of particulars · pre-trial motions · appeals · trial of other charges · period while matters under court consideration
(b) Continuance granted at request of or with consent of defendant or counsel — court may grant only if satisfied postponement is in the interest of justice. A defendant without counsel must NOT be deemed to have consented unless advised by the court of speedy trial rights AND the effect of consent — ON THE RECORD in open court
(c)(i) Absence or unavailability of defendant — Absent = location unknown AND attempting to avoid apprehension/prosecution, OR location cannot be determined by due diligence. Unavailable = location known but presence for trial cannot be obtained by due diligence
(c)(ii) Defendant escaped custody or failed to appear after release on bail/own recognizance — PROVIDED defendant is not in custody on another matter. Period = from day court issues bench warrant (§530.70) to day defendant subsequently appears pursuant to bench warrant or voluntarily
(d) Reasonable delay when defendant joined for trial with co-defendant as to whom the time has not run — only if no good cause shown for severance
(e) Detention in another jurisdiction — DA must be aware of detention AND must have been diligent and made reasonable efforts to obtain defendant's presence for trial
(f) Period during which defendant is without counsel through no fault of the court — EXCEPT when defendant is proceeding as own attorney with court's permission
(g) Exceptional circumstances — (i) continuance because evidence material to People's case is unavailable: DA exercised due diligence AND reasonable grounds to believe evidence will become available; OR (ii) continuance to allow DA additional prep time justified by exceptional circumstances. Any such exclusion when a statement of unreadiness follows a statement of readiness must be evaluated by court after inquiry on the record, only approved upon sufficient supporting facts
(h) Period during which action is adjourned in contemplation of dismissal (ACD) pursuant to CPL §§170.55, 170.56, and 215.10
(i) Period prior to defendant's actual appearance for arraignment when directed to appear by DA pursuant to CPL §120.20(3) or §210.10(3)
(j) Period during which a family offense is before a Family Court — until an accusatory instrument or indictment is filed alleging a crime constituting a family offense (§530.11)
§30.30(5) — Certificate of Compliance (NEW 2026)
🆕 NEW 2026: Court must inquire on the record as to actual readiness when People state readiness. If not actually ready, statement of readiness is INVALID
Certificate of compliance is INVALID when court finds People did not exercise due diligence — assessed by totality of prosecution's efforts including: volume of discovery provided, complexity of case, whether prosecutor knew of missing material, response when apprised of missing discovery, whether omission was corrected, and whether delayed disclosure was prejudicial to defense
🆕 NEW 2026 — §30.30(5-a): On a local criminal court accusatory instrument, statement of readiness not valid unless prosecuting attorney certifies all counts meet CPL §§100.15 and 100.40 requirements
§30.30(7) — Commencement Rules for Retrials & Instrument Conversions
(a) Retrial/withdrawal of plea: Action deemed to commence on date withdrawal of guilty plea becomes final OR date order occasioning retrial becomes final
(b) Appearance ticket: Criminal action deemed to commence on date defendant first appears in local criminal court in response to ticket
(c) Felony complaint converted → Information / Prosecutor's Info / Misdemeanor Complaint [§30.30(1) non-custody]: Period = period applicable to new instrument, calculated from new filing date — UNLESS aggregate of that period + time already elapsed from felony complaint date (excluding §30.30(4) periods) exceeds 6 months → then original felony complaint period remains applicable
(d) Same conversion [§30.30(2) custody]: Same rule — UNLESS aggregate exceeds 90 days → original felony complaint date controls
(e) Indictment count reduced → misdemeanor/petty offense [§30.30(1)]: Period = period applicable to new reduced instrument from new filing date — UNLESS aggregate exceeds 6 months → indictment date controls
(f) Indictment reduced [§30.30(2) custody]: Same rule — UNLESS aggregate exceeds 90 days → indictment date controls
§30.30(5), (5-a), (6), (8) — Additional Rules
🆕 §30.30(5)(a) NEW 2026: When People state or provide notice of readiness, court SHALL make inquiry on the record as to actual readiness. If court determines People are not ready, statement of readiness is NOT valid
🆕 §30.30(5)(b) NEW 2026: Certificate of compliance is invalid when court finds People did NOT exercise due diligence — assessed by totality of prosecution's efforts considering: volume of discovery provided vs. outstanding · complexity of case · whether prosecutor knew of missing material · explanation for lapse · prosecutor's response when apprised of missing discovery · whether omission was corrected · whether prosecution self-reported and took prompt remedial action · whether delayed disclosure was prejudicial to defense or impeded investigation/trial preparation
🆕 §30.30(5-a) NEW 2026: On a local criminal court accusatory instrument, statement of readiness NOT valid unless prosecuting attorney certifies all counts meet CPL §§100.15 and 100.40 requirements, and non-compliant counts have been dismissed
§30.30(6) Appellate review: Order finally denying a §30.30(1) motion is reviewable on appeal from an ensuing judgment of conviction — even if judgment entered upon a guilty plea
§30.30(8) Hearing burden: Procedural rules of CPL §210.45(1)–(7) do NOT apply to §30.30 motions. If time period is in dispute upon oral argument, court must promptly conduct a hearing — People must PROVE the disputed period is excludable
