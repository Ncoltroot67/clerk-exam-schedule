📋 Study Notes — CPL Art. 10 · Criminal Courts: Enumeration, Definitions & Jurisdiction (2026)
§10.10 — The Two Tiers of Criminal Courts
Superior Courts: Supreme Court OR County Court — ONLY these two
Local Criminal Courts: District Court · NYC Criminal Court · City Court · Town Court · Village Court · Supreme Court Justice sitting as LCC · County Judge sitting as LCC
⚡ NYC Criminal Court ≠ City Court. NYC Criminal Court is its own LCC category. City Court = any city court OTHER than NYC, having trial jurisdiction of offenses less than felony grade only
Town Court = all the town justices of a town. Village Court = village justice(s) — if all absent, an authorized Associate Justice may act
Dual-jurisdiction courts — when exercising civil jurisdiction only, NOT acting as a criminal court. Orders made in civil capacity are NOT orders of a criminal court
§10.20 — Superior Courts: Jurisdiction
Trial jurisdiction — Felonies: EXCLUSIVE — only Superior Courts try felonies
Trial jurisdiction — Misdemeanors: CONCURRENT — shared with Local Criminal Courts
Trial jurisdiction — Petty Offenses: ONLY when charged in an indictment that ALSO charges a crime
⚡ Superior Court preliminary jurisdiction = Grand Jury ONLY. Superior Courts exercise preliminary jurisdiction only through their Grand Juries — not directly
Superior Court judge sitting as LCC (§10.20(3)) — DISCRETIONARY: Limited to (1) conducting arraignments §§170.15(2)/180.20(2), (2) issuing warrants of arrest, (3) issuing search warrants — PRELIMINARY JURISDICTION ONLY, no trial jurisdiction
§10.30 — Local Criminal Courts: Jurisdiction
Felonies: Preliminary jurisdiction ONLY — subject to divestiture. NO trial jurisdiction
Misdemeanors: CONCURRENT trial jurisdiction — subject to divestiture by Superior Court in any particular case
Petty Offenses: EXCLUSIVE trial jurisdiction — except when petty offense charged alongside a crime in a Superior Court indictment
Preliminary jurisdiction: All offenses — subject to divestiture by superior courts and their grand juries
⚡ Divestiture: Superior Court can divest an LCC of misdemeanor jurisdiction in a particular case. LCC preliminary jurisdiction is always subject to divestiture
Superior Court judge sitting as LCC: NO trial jurisdiction of ANY offense — preliminary jurisdiction ONLY regardless of what offense is charged
§10.40 — Chief Administrator: Forms and E-Filing
Forms: Chief Administrator may adopt/amend/rescind forms. Failure to use authorized forms is NOT grounds alone to deny or grant any motion
E-filing: Chief Administrator may promulgate e-filing rules with approval of Administrative Board of Courts — for both commencing actions and pending proceedings
Voluntary program: No consent of other party needed to file. No party may be compelled to participate
Mandatory program: Chief Administrator must first obtain agreement of DA AND Criminal Defense Bar of the county. Reasonable notice and comment opportunity required. Comments posted publicly on OCA website
⚡ Unrepresented parties (pro se): ALWAYS exempt from e-filing — whether voluntary or mandatory. Cannot be compelled to participate under any circumstances
⚡ Criminal e-filed documents are NEVER publicly available online. No paper filed electronically in a criminal proceeding is available for public inspection online. Chief Administrator may post unsealed docs on a separate public UCS website only when in the public interest
Attorney opt-out (mandatory programs): May opt out by certifying lack of computer hardware/internet/conversion device OR lack of requisite knowledge. Court may also exempt for good cause. Knowledge of attorney's employees is imputed to the attorney
Consent of Criminal Defense Bar: Written consent from all provider offices/organizations in the county that represented 25% or more of persons represented by public defense providers (per County Law §722 annual reports)

§30.10(4) — Tolling: The following periods are NOT counted in calculating the limitation period:

  • (a) Any period during which (i) defendant was continuously outside New York, or (ii) defendant's whereabouts were continuously unknown and unascertainable by due diligence — but in no event shall this extension exceed 5 additional years beyond the regular period.

(Example: misdemeanor SOL = 2 years + max 5 = 7 years total; felony SOL = 5 years + max 5 = 10 years total.)

  • (b) The period from commencement of a timely prosecution on the same conduct/offense to dismissal of that accusatory instrument, where dismissal is under circumstances permitting lodging of another charge for the same offense or conduct.
§30.20 — Right to Speedy Trial

Defendant is entitled to a SPEEDY TRIAL after a criminal action is commenced. Criminal cases get preference over civil cases; criminal cases where defendant is committed to the custody of the sheriff get preference over other criminal cases.

§30.30(1) — People's Readiness: Motion to Dismiss Deadlines

★ MOTION TO DISMISS MUST BE GRANTED if the People are not ready for trial within the following periods after filing of the accusatory instrument ★

TYPE OF OFFENSEPEOPLE MUST BE READY WITHIN
Felony (at least one charge)6 months
Misdemeanor "A" — sentence over 3 months (no felony)90 days
Misdemeanor "B" — sentence up to 3 months (no crime punishable by more than 3 months)60 days
Violation — none of which is a crime (§30.30(1)(d))30 days

Note: "Offense" includes VTL infractions.

§30.30(2) — Custody Release Deadlines

★ WHERE DEFENDANT HAS BEEN COMMITTED TO CUSTODY OF SHERIFF (or Office of Children and Family Services), defendant MUST BE RELEASED on bail or ROR when the People are not ready for trial within the following periods after commencement of defendant's custody ★

TYPE OF OFFENSEMUST BE RELEASED WITHIN
Felony (at least one charge)90 days
Misdemeanor "A" — sentence over 3 months (no felony)30 days
Misdemeanor "B" — sentence up to 3 months (no crime punishable by more than 3 months)15 days
Violation — none of which is a crime (§30.30(2)(d))5 days

Note: Release NOT required if defendant: is serving a term for another offense; is held on another charge whose applicable period has not elapsed; or is re-detained after release for a new crime or violation of release conditions.

§30.30(3) — Exceptions

(a) Homicide exception: §30.30(1) and (2) do NOT apply where any offense charged is defined in PL §§125.10, 125.15, 125.20, 125.25, 125.26, or 125.27.

(b) Exceptional circumstance: A motion MAY be denied if the People were previously ready but are now unready due to exceptional facts — including sudden unavailability of material evidence — when D.A. has exercised due diligence and there are reasonable grounds evidence will become available in a reasonable period.

(c) §30.30(2) release shall NOT: (i) apply to defendant serving a term for another offense; (ii) require release of defendant held on another charge whose period has not elapsed; (iii) prevent re-detention after release for a new crime or violation of release conditions.

§30.30(4) — Excluded Periods (Tolls Against Readiness Clock)
  • (a) Delay from other proceedings: competency determination/period; demand to produce; bill of particulars; pre-trial motions; appeals; trial of other charges; court consideration thereof.
  • (b) Continuance at defendant's request or with defendant's consent — court must be satisfied it is in the interest of justice; pro-se defendant must be advised on the record.
  • (c)(i) Absence or unavailability of defendant. Absent = location unknown, attempting to avoid apprehension/prosecution, or location undeterminable by due diligence. Unavailable = location known but presence cannot be obtained by due diligence.
  • (c)(ii) Defendant escaped or failed to appear on bail/ROR (not in custody on another matter): from bench warrant issuance (§530.70) to defendant's subsequent appearance.
  • (d) Reasonable delay for co-defendant joinder where good cause is not shown for severance.
  • (e) Detention in another jurisdiction where D.A. is aware and making diligent reasonable efforts.
  • (f) Period without counsel through no fault of the court (except when defendant proceeds pro-se with court permission).
  • (g) Exceptional circumstances: (i) unavailability of material evidence — D.A. exercised due diligence, reasonable grounds evidence will become available; or (ii) additional time justified by exceptional case circumstances. After a prior statement of readiness, requires court inquiry on the record.
  • (h) Period of ACD/ACOD (§§170.55, 170.56, or 215.10).
  • (i) Period prior to defendant's actual arraignment appearance where defendant was directed to appear by D.A. (§120.20(3) or §210.10(3)).
  • (j) Period while family offense is before Family Court — until accusatory instrument or indictment is filed alleging a crime constituting a family offense (§530.11).
§30.30(5) — Readiness Statements & Certificate of Compliance

(a) Court must inquire on the record as to actual readiness whenever the People state or notice readiness. If not actually ready, the statement is invalid. Court may deem People NOT ready for failure to comply with Article 245 (discovery) ONLY if the certificate of compliance was invalid under §245.50 — assessed by totality of prosecution's efforts, considering: efforts made; volume of discovery provided and outstanding; case complexity; knowledge of missing material; explanation for lapse; response upon notice; whether belated discovery was duplicative/insignificant/easily remedied; self-reporting and prompt remedial action; and whether delayed disclosure was prejudicial.

(b) On a local criminal court accusatory instrument, readiness statement is INVALID unless the prosecutor certifies all counts meet §§100.15 and 100.40, and non-compliant counts have been dismissed.

§30.30(6)

An order finally denying a motion to dismiss under §30.30(1) is reviewable on appeal from an ensuing judgment of conviction — even if entered upon a guilty plea.

§30.30(7) — Special Commencement Rules
  • (a) After withdrawal of guilty plea or order for retrial: criminal action deemed commenced on the date the withdrawal or order becomes final.
  • (b) After appearance ticket served: action deemed commenced on date of defendant's first appearance in local criminal court.
  • (c) After felony complaint converted/replaced by information, prosecutor's information, or misdemeanor complaint: §30.30(1) period runs from the new instrument — UNLESS aggregate of new period plus elapsed time exceeds 6 months, in which case the felony complaint period controls.
  • (d) Same as (c) for §30.30(2) custody-release period — UNLESS aggregate exceeds 90 days.
  • (e) After count of indictment reduced to misdemeanor/petty offense and reduced indictment or prosecutor's information filed: §30.30(1) period runs from new instrument — UNLESS aggregate exceeds 6 months.
  • (f) Same as (e) for §30.30(2) custody-release period — UNLESS aggregate exceeds 90 days.
§30.30(8)

The procedural rules of §210.45(1)-(7) governing motions to dismiss an indictment do NOT apply to a §30.30(2) motion. If a time period is in dispute upon oral argument, the court must promptly conduct a hearing at which the People must prove the period is excludable.

CPL Art. 100

§§100.05–100.60 · Accusatory Instruments
§100.05 — Commencement of Action; In General

A criminal action is commenced by the filing of an accusatory instrument with a criminal court. For a juvenile offender or adolescent offender (other than one charged only with a violation or traffic infraction), commencement is in the youth part of the superior court. If multiple instruments are filed in the same action, commencement dates from the FIRST filing.

The ONLY way to commence a criminal action in a Superior Court (non-juvenile) is by the Grand Jury filing an indictment against a defendant who has NEVER been held by a local criminal court for the action of that grand jury on any charge in the indictment.

Otherwise, a local criminal court action commences by filing: Information; Simplified Information; Prosecutor's Information; Misdemeanor Complaint; or Felony Complaint.

§100.07 — Family Court; Concurrent Jurisdiction

Criminal Courts have concurrent jurisdiction with Family Court over family offense proceedings, even where Family Court has or may be exercising jurisdiction over substantially the same allegations.

§100.10 — Accusatory Instruments; Definitions

§100.10(1) — Information — verified written accusation by a person, filed with a local criminal court, charging one or more offenses — NONE of which is a felony. May both commence an action and serve as the basis for prosecution.

§100.10(2) — Simplified Information — three types (written accusations in simplified form, containing NO factual allegations of an evidentiary nature, charging only non-felony offenses):

  • (a) Simplified Traffic Information — Commissioner of Motor Vehicles form; charges traffic infractions and/or misdemeanors relating to traffic.
  • (b) Simplified Parks Information — Commissioner of Parks and Recreation form; charges non-felony offenses prosecutable under the Parks and Recreation Law/Navigation Law.
  • (c) Simplified Environmental Conservation Information — Commissioner of Environmental Conservation form; charges non-felony offenses under the Environmental Conservation Law.

All three commence actions and may serve as basis for prosecution under §100.25 circumstances.

§100.10(3) — Prosecutor's Information — written accusation by the D.A., filed with a local criminal court, issued at: (a) direction of a Grand Jury (§190.70); (b) direction of a local criminal court (§§180.50 or 180.70); (c) D.A.'s own instance (§100.50(2)); or (d) direction of a superior court (§210.20(1-a)). Charges only non-felony offenses. Serves as the basis for prosecution, but commences an action ONLY where it results from a Grand Jury direction in a case NOT previously commenced in a local criminal court.

§100.10(4) — Misdemeanor Complaint — verified written accusation charging at least one misdemeanor and no felonies. Commences an action; may serve as prosecution basis ONLY where defendant WAIVES prosecution by information (§170.65(3)).

§100.10(5) — Felony Complaint — verified written accusation filed with a local criminal court OR youth part of the superior court, charging one or more felonies. Commences an action; NEVER serves as a basis for prosecution.

§100.15 — Form and Content

All informations and complaints must: specify the name of the court and title of the action; be subscribed and verified by the complainant (including upon "information and belief"). Contains two parts — complainant's verification applies ONLY to the Factual Part:

  • Factual Part — statement of evidentiary facts supporting the charges; where multiple offenses are charged, should be a single account applicable to all counts. Allegations may be based on personal knowledge or information and belief. Non-hearsay requirement for sufficiency of an Information (§100.40(1)(c)) applies separately.
  • Accusatory Part — designates each offense in separate counts. May charge two or more defendants ONLY if all are jointly charged with every offense alleged.

Where a felony complaint charges a violent felony (PL §70.02) that is also an armed felony (CPL §1.20(41)): the accusatory part MUST designate it as an armed felony, and the factual part must allege supporting facts.

§100.20 — Supporting Deposition

A verified written instrument subscribed by a person OTHER than the complainant, accompanying or filed with an Information, Simplified Information, Misdemeanor Complaint, or Felony Complaint. Contains factual allegations of an evidentiary character (based on personal knowledge or information and belief) that supplement the accusatory instrument and support or tend to support the charges.

§100.25 — Simplified Information: Supporting Deposition Rights

(1) Must substantially conform to the form prescribed by the applicable Commissioner.

(2) Right to Supporting Deposition — Timely Request: Defendant is entitled as a matter of right, upon timely request, to a supporting deposition from the complainant officer/public servant providing reasonable cause to believe defendant committed the offense(s). Timely = before guilty plea or trial AND no later than 30 days after the appearance date. If mailed, must be mailed within such 30-day period.

Upon request, court must order officer to: serve the deposition within 30 days of receipt of the request, or at least 5 days before trial, whichever is EARLIER; and file it with the clerk with proof of service.

(3) Where at least one charge is a misdemeanor, court may permit a late request for good cause in the interest of justice — but NO motion may be brought more than 90 days after the appearance date.

(4) Notice on appearance ticket — must contain verbatim:

"NOTICE: YOU ARE ENTITLED TO RECEIVE A SUPPORTING DEPOSITION FURTHER EXPLAINING THE CHARGES PROVIDED YOU REQUEST SUCH SUPPORTING DEPOSITION WITHIN THIRTY DAYS FROM THE DATE YOU ARE DIRECTED TO APPEAR IN COURT AS SET FORTH ON THIS APPEARANCE TICKET. DO YOU REQUEST A SUPPORTING DEPOSITION? [ ] YES [ ] NO"
§100.30 — Verification Methods

Verification of an Information, Misdemeanor Complaint, Felony Complaint, Supporting Deposition, or proof of service thereof may be by any of the following (unless court directs a specific method):

  • (a) Sworn before the court where filed;
  • (b) Sworn before a police desk officer or superior officer;
  • (c) Where filed by a public servant after issuance of an appearance ticket, sworn before another designated public servant authorized by law;
  • (d) Instrument bears a notice that false statements are punishable as a Class A Misdemeanor (PL §210.45) — subscription together with the notice constitutes verification;
  • (e) Sworn before a notary public.
§100.35 — Prosecutor's Information: Form and Content

Must contain: name of the local criminal court and title of action; subscribed by the D.A.; one or more counts with a plain and concise statement of conduct for each offense. Otherwise follows indictment form (§200.50). Joinder rules for indictments (§§200.20 and 200.40) apply.

§100.40 — Facial Sufficiency

Information (or count): sufficient when (a) substantially conforms to §100.15; (b) factual allegations and/or supporting depositions provide reasonable cause to believe defendant committed the offense; AND (c) non-hearsay allegations establish, if true, every element of the offense and defendant's commission thereof.

Simplified Information: sufficient when substantially conforms to prescribed form. Failure by complainant officer to comply with a court order for a supporting deposition within the time provided by §100.25(2) renders it INSUFFICIENT on its face.

Prosecutor's Information (or count): sufficient when substantially conforms to §100.35.

Misdemeanor/Felony Complaint (or count): sufficient when (a) substantially conforms to §100.15; AND (b) allegations provide reasonable cause to believe defendant committed the offense — NO non-hearsay requirement.

§100.45 — Severance, Amendment, Bill of Particulars

Severance, consolidation (§§200.20, 200.40), and bills of particulars (§200.95) apply to Informations, Prosecutor's Informations, and Misdemeanor Complaints.

Amendment of Prosecutor's Information governed by §200.70 indictment amendment rules.

Amendment of Information: before guilty plea or trial, court may — on application of the People with notice to defendant and opportunity to be heard — add a count supported by existing factual allegations/supporting depositions. Defendant must receive a reasonable adjournment.

§100.50 — Superseding Informations and Prosecutor's Informations

(1) If, before guilty plea or trial, another Information or Prosecutor's Information is filed charging an offense already in the first instrument, the second SUPERSEDES the first as to that offense; upon arraignment on the second, the corresponding count of the first MUST be dismissed. The first is NOT superseded as to counts not charged in the second.

(2) Before guilty plea or trial, D.A. may file a Prosecutor's Information charging any offense supported by the original Information's factual part and/or supporting depositions; the original is superseded and, upon arraignment on the Prosecutor's Information, DEEMED DISMISSED.

(3) A misdemeanor complaint must or may be replaced and superseded by an information under §170.65.

§100.55 — Where Local Criminal Court Instruments May Be Filed
  • (1) District Court: any county where offense was allegedly committed (or that part within the court's jurisdiction).
  • (2) NYC Criminal Court: any offense allegedly committed in New York City.
  • (3) City Court: any offense allegedly committed in that city.
  • (4) Town Court: Information, Simplified Information, Prosecutor's Information, or Misdemeanor Complaint — when offense was allegedly committed anywhere in that town OTHER THAN in a village thereof having a village court.
  • (5) Village Court: Information, Simplified Information, Prosecutor's Information, or Misdemeanor Complaint — when offense was allegedly committed in that village.
  • (6) Felony Complaint: any town or village court of the county where the felony was allegedly committed — need NOT be the specific town/village of commission.
  • (7) Superior Court Judge sitting as local court: Information, Simplified Information, Misdemeanor Complaint, or Felony Complaint — when offense allegedly committed in a county where judge is then present AND either resides or is currently holding (or assigned to hold) a term of superior court.
  • (8) Other courts as expressly authorized by law.
  • (9) Where two or more courts are each authorized: may file with ANY ONE, but NOT MORE THAN ONE.
  • (10) "Committed in" includes where the offense is prosecutable under §§20.40 and 20.50.
  • (11) May also be filed with a local criminal court operating an OFF-HOURS ARRAIGNMENT PART (Judiciary Law §212(1)(w)), provided the offense was allegedly committed in that court's county.
§100.60 — Youth Part: Where Instruments May Be Filed

Any youth part of the superior court accusatory instrument may be filed with the youth part of the superior court of the particular county in which the offense charged was allegedly committed (or the part thereof within the court's jurisdiction).

CPL Art. 110

§§110.10–110.20 · Arraignment
§110.10 — Methods of Requiring Defendant's Appearance for Arraignment

After a criminal action has been commenced in a local criminal court or youth part of the superior court by filing an accusatory instrument, a defendant not yet arraigned and not under the court's control may be compelled or required to appear for arraignment by:

  • (a) Issuance and execution of a warrant of arrest (Article 120); or
  • (b) Issuance and service of a summons (Article 130); or
  • (c) Interstate/extradition procedures under Articles 560, 570, 580, 590, and 600 (for defendants not at liberty within the state).

Where NO criminal action has yet been commenced, a defendant may be compelled to appear in a local criminal court or youth part of the superior court for arraignment upon an instrument to be filed at or before the time of appearance, by:

  • (a) Warrantless arrest (Article 140); or
  • (b) Issuance and service of an appearance ticket (Article 150).
§110.20 — Notice to District Attorney

Whenever a criminal action in which a crime is charged is commenced in a local criminal court or youth part of the superior court (other than the NYC Criminal Court), a copy of the accusatory instrument must be promptly transmitted to the appropriate District Attorney upon or prior to arraignment.

  • If a police officer or peace officer is the complainant or filer of a Simplified Information, or has arrested the defendant or brought him before the court pursuant to §140.20(1): that officer or agency transmits the copy to the D.A.
  • In all other cases: the clerk of the arraigning court transmits it.

CPL Art. 120

§§120.10–120.90 · Warrant of Arrest
§120.10 — Definition, Form and Content

§120.10(1) — A warrant of arrest is process issued by a local criminal court directing a police officer to arrest a defendant designated in an accusatory instrument filed with such court and to bring him before that court. Its SOLE function is to achieve the defendant's court appearance in a criminal action for the purpose of arraignment upon the accusatory instrument by which the action was commenced.

§120.10(2) — Warrants must be subscribed by the issuing judge and must state:

  • (a) Name of issuing court;
  • (b) Date of issuance;
  • (c) Name or title of an offense charged in the underlying accusatory instrument;
  • (d) Defendant's name, or if unknown, any name or description by which he can be identified with reasonable certainty;
  • (e) The police officer(s) to whom addressed; and
  • (f) A direction that such officer arrest the defendant and bring him before the issuing court.

§120.10(3) — A warrant may be addressed to a classification of police officers (or two or more classifications), as well as to a designated individual officer or officers. Multiple copies may be issued.

§120.20 — When Issuable

When a criminal action has been commenced in a local criminal court or youth part of the superior court by filing an accusatory instrument other than a simplified traffic information, against a defendant not yet arraigned and not under the court's control:

  • (a) The court MAY issue a warrant if the accusatory instrument is sufficient on its face (per §100.40); OR
  • (b) If the instrument is NOT sufficient on its face AND it would be IMPOSSIBLE to file a sufficient one based on available facts/evidence, the court MUST dismiss the accusatory instrument.

Even where sufficient, the court may REFUSE to issue a warrant and may further examine witnesses (under oath or otherwise) to satisfy itself there is reasonable cause to believe defendant committed the charged offense.

Notwithstanding subd. 1: if a summons may be issued under §130.20 and the court is satisfied defendant will respond, it MAY NOT issue a warrant. Upon the D.A.'s request, in lieu of a warrant or summons, the court may authorize the D.A. to direct the defendant to appear for arraignment on a designated date.

§120.30 — By What Courts Issuable; Returnable

A warrant may be issued ONLY by the local criminal court or youth part of the superior court with which the underlying accusatory instrument has been filed (as determined by §100.55 or §100.60), and is returnable ONLY in that issuing court.

Backup filing rules:

  • If the target town court is unavailable: instrument may be filed with the town court of any adjoining town in the same county.
  • If the target village court is unavailable: with the town court embracing that village, or if that is also unavailable, with the town court of any adjoining town in the same county.
§120.40 — Attaching Accusatory Instrument to Warrant

A town court, village court, or city court issuing a warrant may attach a duplicate copy of the underlying accusatory instrument to the warrant (and to any duplicate copies of the warrant). Where a defendant is brought before a different court under §120.90(5), the attached copy constitutes a valid basis for arraignment under §170.15(1).

§120.50 — To Whom Warrant Is Addressed

A warrant shall be addressed to any police officer or classification of police officers whose geographical area of employment embraces EITHER (a) the place where the offense was allegedly committed, OR (b) the locality of the issuing court.

§120.55 — Execution by Parole / Probation Officers

If a defendant named in a warrant issued by a local criminal court, youth part of the superior court, or a superior court (§210.10(3)) is under supervision of the State Department of Corrections and Community Supervision or a local or state probation department, the warrant may be executed by a parole officer or probation officer — when authorized by his or her probation director — within the officer's geographical area of employment, in the same manner and upon the same conditions as execution by a police officer.

§120.60 — What Police Officers May Execute a Warrant

A warrant may be executed by: (a) any police officer to whom it is addressed; or (b) any other police officer delegated to execute it as an agent.

Delegation — Conditions (subd. 2): ALL of the following must be met:

  • (a) Addressee has reasonable cause to believe defendant is in a county other than the one where the warrant is returnable; AND
  • (b) The warrant is executable in that county without endorsement (§120.70); AND
  • (c) The geographical area of employment of the delegated officer embraces the locality of arrest.

Delegation — Procedure (subd. 3): The addressee may inform the delegated officer — by telecommunication, mail, or any other means — of the warrant's issuance, the offense charged, and all other pertinent details, and may request the delegated officer to act as agent. Upon such request, the delegated officer has the same authority as the delegating officer within the delegated officer's geographical area of employment. Upon arresting the defendant, the delegated officer must proceed as provided in §120.90 subdivisions two and four.

§120.70 — Where Warrants Are Executable

Statewide: Warrants issued by a District Court, NYC Criminal Court, youth part of a superior court, or a Superior Court judge sitting as a local criminal court may be executed ANYWHERE IN THE STATE.

City/Town/Village court warrants: executable in the county of issuance or any ADJOINING county; or ANYWHERE else in the state upon the written endorsement of a local criminal court of the county where the arrest is to be made — upon endorsement, the warrant is deemed the process of BOTH the issuing and endorsing courts.

§120.80 — When and How Executed

Warrants may be executed on ANY day of the week and at ANY hour of the day or night.

Unless encountering physical resistance, flight, or other factors rendering normal procedure impractical, officer MUST inform the defendant a warrant for his arrest has been issued for the designated offense; must show the warrant upon request if in possession, or at the earliest opportunity thereafter.

Officer may use physical force justifiable under PL §35.30.

Officer may enter any premises where defendant is reasonably believed present — but if a THIRD PARTY'S dwelling, must proceed per Article 690. Before entry, must give or make reasonable effort to give notice of authority and purpose — UNLESS there is reasonable cause to believe notice would result in: (a) defendant escaping; (b) endangering life/safety of officer or another; or (c) destruction/damage/concealment of material evidence. If authorized to enter without notice, or if notice is given and entry denied, officer may enter by breaking if necessary.

§120.85 — Child-Sensitive Arrests

A state or local law enforcement officer who arrests an individual legally charged with the care or custody of a child under 18 must, to the extent practicable at the time of arrest, take appropriate action conducive to the child's safety and/or security, consistent with policies established under Executive Law §214-i or §840(3)(f-2), as applicable.

§120.90 — Procedure After Arrest on a Warrant

(1) Addressee — same/adjoining county, or felony anywhere in state: Must without unnecessary delay bring defendant before the local criminal court or youth part of the superior court in which the warrant is returnable. If an off-hours arraignment part (Judiciary Law §212(1)(w)) is operating in that county at the time, officer may bring defendant there instead.

(2) Delegated officer — same/adjoining county or felony: Must without unnecessary delay deliver defendant to the delegating officer, who then proceeds as in subd. 1.

(3) Addressee — non-felony, non-adjoining county: Must inform defendant of right to appear before a local criminal court of the county of arrest for ROR or bail.

  • If defendant does NOT wish to exercise this right: officer requests him to endorse such fact upon the warrant; on endorsement, officer brings defendant before the issuing court.
  • If defendant DOES wish to exercise (or refuses to endorse): officer must bring defendant before a local court of the county of arrest, which must release on ROR or fix bail for appearance in the issuing court on a specified date.
  • If defendant is in default of bail: officer must bring defendant before the issuing court.

(4) Delegated officer — non-felony, non-adjoining county: May hold defendant up to 2 HOURS to deliver to the delegating officer.

  • If not delivered in time: delegated officer must inform defendant of right to appear before a local court for ROR or bail.
  • If defendant does NOT wish to exercise: officer requests him to make, sign, and deliver a written statement of such fact; officer retains custody and delivers to delegating officer.
  • If defendant DOES wish to exercise (or refuses to make/deliver the statement): officer must bring defendant before a local court of the county of arrest and submit a written statement reciting material facts; that court releases on ROR or fixes bail. Default of bail: officer retains custody, delivers to delegating officer, who brings defendant before the issuing court.

(5) Alternate court when issuing court unavailable (instrument must be attached per §120.40):

  • Town court unavailable: bring before any village court embraced (in whole or in part) by such town, or the local criminal court of an adjoining town or city in the same county, or a village court of such adjoining town.
  • Village court unavailable: bring before the town court of the town embracing such village, or any other village court within such town; if those are also unavailable, before the local criminal court of any adjoining town or city of the same county, or any village embraced in whole or in part by such adjoining town.
  • City court unavailable: bring before the local criminal court of any adjoining town or village embraced in whole or in part by such adjoining town of the same county.

(5-a) Youth Part unavailable: If defendant is required to be brought before a youth part of a superior court and that court is not in session, officer must bring defendant before the most accessible magistrate designated by the Appellate Division of the applicable department to act as a youth part.

(6) Fingerprinting: Officer must perform all required fingerprinting and other preliminary police duties before bringing defendant before court. Where defendant was released by another county's local court before fingerprinting (for an offense specified in §160.10(1)), the arraigning court must direct defendant to appear for fingerprinting at a designated time and place.

(7) Juvenile/Adolescent Offenders: Upon arrest of a juvenile offender or adolescent offender, officer must immediately notify the parent or other person legally responsible for the offender's care (or person with whom offender is domiciled) of the arrest and the location of the detention facility.

(8) Telephone access: Upon arresting a defendant other than a juvenile offender for any offense pursuant to a warrant, officer must — upon defendant's request — permit the defendant to communicate by telephone (provided by the law enforcement facility) to any number in the United States or Puerto Rico to obtain counsel and/or inform a relative or friend of the arrest — UNLESS granting the call will compromise an ongoing investigation or prosecution.

§10.00(18) — Juvenile Offender (Two Tiers)

Tier 1 — Age 13: Criminally responsible for acts constituting Murder in the Second Degree (PL §125.25(1) or (2)) or such conduct as a sexually motivated felony (PL §130.91).

Tier 2 — Age 14 or 15: Criminally responsible for acts constituting:

  • Murder 2° — §125.25(1) and (2); or §125.25(3) where the underlying crime is one for which such person is criminally responsible;
  • Kidnapping 1° (§135.25); Arson 1° (§150.20); Assault 1° (§120.10(1) and (2)); Manslaughter 1° (§125.20);
  • Rape 1° — §130.35, paragraphs (a) and (b) of subdivisions one, two, and three; or former §130.35 subdivisions one and two;
  • Former Criminal Sexual Act 1° — former §130.50 subdivisions one and two;
  • Aggravated Sexual Abuse 1° (§130.70); Burglary 1° (§140.30); Burglary 2° subd. 1 (§140.25(1)); Arson 2° (§150.15);
  • Robbery 1° (§160.15); Robbery 2° subd. 2 (§160.10(2));
  • §265.03 (Criminal Possession of a Weapon — machine gun or firearm) where such machine gun or firearm is possessed on school grounds as defined in PL §220.00(14); or
  • An attempt to commit Murder 2° or Kidnapping 1°, or such conduct as a sexually motivated felony (PL §130.91).
